I'm glad to share that a new article has been published in _Advanced Functional Materials_.

This work, entitled _Color Routing and Beam Steering of Single-Molecule Emission with a Spherical Silicon Nanoantenna_, has been carried out in a joint international effort (University of Fribourg, Switzerland, University of Cantabria, Spain; and Kobe University, Japan) led by María Sanz-Paz in Guillermo Acuña's Photonic Nanosystems Group (University of Fribourg). Here, DNA origami scaffolds are used to position single fluorophores in nanometric proximity to a single silicon spherical nanoparticle. Due to the selective excitation of electric and magnetic Mie resonances, we demonstrate unidirectional emission with high forward-to-backward intensity ratios. Furthermore, we have shown that a single silicon nanosphere antenna can function as a color router or a beam steerer depending on its size, emitter spectral range and emitter-nanoparticle distance, enabling the use of these structures as versatile functional components in photonic devices.
